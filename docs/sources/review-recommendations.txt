VERDICT


Keep the boundary. Price the shared
work.
Review of Federated Swarm Hackathon Platform, System Specification v1.0, September 2026, 22 pages. Separate
recommendations; the original is unchanged.



This is a strong architecture. The edge/center boundary is correctly drawn: participants may compute
freely, while the center governs shared pressure and canonical state. Keep that idea. The gaps are in the
policies that make it hold at the stated scale, not in the basic model.

What is already right

  Keep                                          Why it matters

  Edge freedom; governed center                 Limit shared attention and shared services, never private compute.
  §§2.3, 14, 34 (pp. 4, 10-11, 21)              Large private swarms need not become large public queues.

  L0-L4 authority ladder                        Publishing is not verification; verification is not canonical authority.
  §10 (pp. 8-9)                                 Preserve those separate gates.

  Load as operational metadata                  Coordination load is not a social score. Keep resource accounting
  §14 (p. 11)                                   private, explainable and surface-specific.

  Moderation in the first deployment            Rate limits, lease revocation, quarantine and bans belong in the
  §32 (pp. 19-20)                               closed loop, not in a later cleanup release.


Four changes before a full-scale event
1. Put validator work on a real budget. 2. Replace parallel stale-base failure with a sequenced merge
queue. 3. Make admission budgets resistant to account multiplication. 4. Define competing-candidate
adjudication and make peer replication worth doing.
The spec already recognizes integration compute, identity multiplication and speculative batching. These
recommendations turn those acknowledgments into enforceable policy. They do not propose a second architecture.

Reading this review
Section and page references point to the supplied v1.0 PDF. Capacity arithmetic is illustrative, not measured
performance. Proposed defaults are design choices to test, not claims about existing behavior.

01 / VALIDATION ECONOMICS


Make shared validation a budgeted
service.
References: §§11-12 (pp. 9-10); §14 (pp. 10-11); §§28, 31-32 (pp. 18-20).



Problem
The Governor counts submissions and tracks integration compute, but does not define a unit, reservation
rule or hard validator budget. Trusted-runner checks are the likely central cost center. Free private
compute does not make merge simulation, smoke tests or canonical builds free.

Why it breaks at the target
At §31's 100 submissions/minute, a mean of 5 core-minutes per admitted candidate needs about
500 continuously busy cores; at 10, about 1,000, before headroom or retries. These are explicit
assumptions, not benchmarks. Admission filters, selective tests and batching may reduce the rate or
cost. Advertised participant nodes are not guaranteed trusted-runner capacity.

Concrete recommendation

  Add                               Implement

  Validation credits                Separate from attention envelopes. Define credits in runner resource units,
                                    with measured CPU/GPU time, memory class and execution deadlines. Credits
                                    can be event-funded; cash pricing is optional.

  Reserve, settle, cap              At L2 admission, estimate and reserve credits; settle against measured use.
                                    Require a hard job cap. A higher estimate needs a new reservation. Distinguish
                                    platform-caused reruns from contributor-caused failures.

  Budget-aware scheduling           Use risk, unblock value, age and cost. Reserve capacity for urgent fixes and
                                    canonical promotion; age waiting jobs to prevent starvation. Reject cheap
                                    garbage before charging expensive execution.

  Explicit degraded state           Publish VALIDATION_BUDGET_CONSTRAINED with reason, remaining capacity
                                    and next eligibility time. Defer costly checks or queue candidates; never
                                    silently weaken a required evidence gate.


Acceptance test
Drive 100 submissions/minute with costly and cheap candidates. Runner spend stays inside the
configured budget; queue growth remains visible; urgent fixes retain reserved capacity; failed and
timed-out jobs cannot consume unbounded resources. Validate that private L0 work remains
unrestricted.
Record estimated/actual credits, cache hits, queue age and platform-caused rerun cost. Benchmark the actual test mix
before sizing the fleet.

02 / CANONICAL THROUGHPUT


Queue the merge, not the invalidation.
References: §8 (pp. 7-8); §§12-13 (pp. 9-10); §§30-31 (pp. 18-19).



Problem
The §13 rule that promotion fails when HEAD changes after validation began is a correct safety guard,
but a poor scheduling strategy on its own. Parallel validations against one moving HEAD can lose their
promotion eligibility every time another candidate lands.

Why it breaks at the target
With 1,000 work cells and typical 10-20 minute leases, one candidate per cell per lease would yield
roughly 50-100 candidates/minute. That is an illustrative turnover scenario, not a lease completion
guarantee. Any validation window containing a promotion can invalidate old-head results under the
current rule, even if changes do not conflict. The spec does not supply promotion frequency or validation
duration, so the failure rate is not yet quantifiable.

Concrete recommendation

  Stage                            Rule

  Admission / triage               Retain cheap candidate checks and risk-aware scheduling. Record the
                                   immutable source candidate and its tested base separately from the queued
                                   merge revision.

  Integration queue                Select an item or compatible batch; rebase or merge it onto the queue's current
                                   parent; validate that exact resulting tree. One authority advances the ordered
                                   frontier.

  Promotion                        Use compare-and-swap on the exact validated parent and record the result
                                   tree, policy version and evidence. Keep the existing single-authority and
                                   rollback protections.

  Speculation                      Allow parallel look-ahead validation only against explicit queue parents. A failed
                                   or removed parent invalidates descendants; requeue them. For a failed batch,
                                   split and isolate the offending change.

  Risk-class reuse                 For R0-R1, reuse isolated evidence only when declared inputs and
                                   dependencies are unchanged. For R2-R5, require the constitution's integration
                                   checks on the actual target tree. Never promote an unvalidated tree.


Acceptance test
Run mixed conflicting and disjoint candidates while main advances. Every promotion points to the exact
validated tree and parent. Measure invalidations per promotion, repeated runner cost, queue latency
and batch failure recovery. Rollback invalidates dependent speculative work and resumes the queue
safely.
Formalize the existing speculative-batch idea rather than relaxing compare-and-swap. Staleness tolerance belongs to
evidence reuse policy, not to canonical identity.

03-04 / ADMISSION AND INCENTIVES


Close the identity loop. Choose
winners explicitly.
References: §§5, 8, 11-15 (pp. 6-12); §§28-29 (p. 18).



03 / Account multiplication defeats per-identity limits
§15 names identity multiplication, and §5 links agents to human or organization owners. What is missing
is an enforceable budget boundary across human accounts. At 100 candidates/minute, ten accounts
allowed ten submissions each could exhaust the target rate. This is an illustrative policy setting; the
actual example in §14 differs.

  Policy                           Concrete recommendation

  Budget inheritance               Charge every subordinate credential and agent to its verified owner.
                                   Organization members consume a shared organization ceiling as well as their
                                   own envelope. New accounts must not automatically reset consumed capacity.

  Entry and ramp                   For the first event, use sponsor invitations plus a small starter validation
                                   allocation. Increase it with independently verified useful work. If permissionless
                                   entry is needed, consider escrow with explicit refund and appeal rules; do not
                                   pretend OAuth alone proves one human.

  Privacy and fairness             Keep abuse linkage private. A shared network address is a signal, not identity
                                   proof. Provide manual review for shared labs and households; budget
                                   restrictions must be explainable and appealable.


04 / Verification does not settle competing solutions
Two L3 candidates for one Work Item can both pass tests. §§12 and 28 expose conflicts and duplicates
but do not define who wins. Meanwhile PEER_REPLICATED evidence has no stated reward or assignment
rule, so relying on volunteer replication may leave trusted runners doing the work.
Adjudication: declare the selection policy on each Work Item before competition starts. Default to the
earliest qualifying L3 candidate, subject to current-base integration and constitution compliance. Use
preregistered quality metrics for comparative tasks; use a named maintainer and decision deadline
where the oracle is incomplete. Record the winning evidence and reason; resolve tied timestamps
deterministically by candidate ID. Losing work becomes superseded, with history intact.
Replication: issue bounded validation-credit rewards for assigned, accepted reproductions. Bind each
attestation to candidate, base, environment, suite and result; exclude self-replication and same-owner
nodes from independent evidence. Random assignment, trusted spot-checks and event reward caps limit
collusion. Rewards increase access to shared validation, not canonical authority.
Test: extra accounts cannot multiply budgets; two qualifying candidates yield one auditable decision; colluding
replicators cannot mint unlimited credits. Peer evidence never bypasses risk-class promotion rules.

05 / SMALLER NOTES AND DELIVERY ORDER


Right-size the lease. Name the oracle.
References: §§7-8 (pp. 7-8); §11 (p. 9); §26 (p. 17); §§31-32, 35 (pp. 19-21).



Lease duration should match the work
A 10-20 minute lease suits a small fix, not every task. Renewal by visible progress can punish a hard task
before it produces a commit, while repeated lease challenges add review load. Leases are coordination
signals, not exclusive ownership; preserve that distinction.
Add a Work Item task class, expected effort band and checkpoint plan. Either decompose work
into lease-sized units or use longer class-specific leases with bounded renewals. Accept milestones such
as a reproduced failure, design artifact or dependency finding. Add challenge cooldowns and evidence
thresholds; private parallel work stays allowed. Measure expiry/challenge rates by class before setting
durations.

Generalization requires a usable oracle
§35's domains vary in how cheaply a result can be checked. Build success and a gameplay smoke test do
not prove every acceptance criterion. Without cheap, machine-checkable evidence for routine
promotion, the center becomes a human-review bottleneck rather than a small coordination layer.
Require each Work Item to name its oracle, reproducible test inputs, pass threshold, check cost
and unresolved human judgment. Classify checks as automated, hybrid or human-only. Give
human-only work a separate review budget and narrower throughput promise. Generalize the protocol
freely; generalize the scale claim only after measuring oracle cost and reliability.

Priority ordering

  When                              Ship / prove

  P0 / Before the first event       Merge-queue semantics and bounded validator execution. Add credit
                                    reservations and explicit budget-constrained admission. These protect
                                    canonical safety and central cost together.

  P0 / Before open signup           Owner-level budget inheritance, invite/ramp policy and appeal path. Set the
                                    per-Work-Item adjudication default. Keep the existing moderation controls in
                                    the first deployment.

  P1 / Before the 1,000-cell        Benchmark runner demand; test queue invalidation and rollback; add guarded
  run                               replication rewards and class-aware leases. Load-test the
                                    100-submissions/minute target.

  P2 / Before new domains           Publish oracle requirements and measured human-review capacity. Do not
                                    export software-event throughput claims to domains whose validation
                                    economics differ.


Bottom line: keep unlimited thinking at the edge. Make every scarce central resource explicit, every
canonical promotion reproducible, and every selection decision auditable.
